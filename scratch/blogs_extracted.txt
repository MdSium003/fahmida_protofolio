An Unplanned Journey from Singapore to Malaysia

Sometimes, the trips we remember the most are not the ones we plan for months.

Sometimes, they begin with a simple sentence:

“What do we have to lose? Let’s just go.”

And that is exactly how our Malaysia trip began.

We Had Planned It. Then We Almost Gave Up.

Even before going to Singapore, I had already decided that we would visit Malaysia.

I kept thinking, Malaysia was so close, and we could get there by bus for very little money. Why would I miss the chance?

The plan was to spend the last few days of our Singapore stay exploring Malaysia before coming back.

But as those final days approached, things became complicated.

Money was getting tight, and honestly, our financial situation wasn't exactly comfortable anymore. My husband didn't really want to go. I was disappointed, because I had been looking forward to this little adventure for so long.

The better bus tickets were around 30 SGD or more, which meant quite a lot for the two of us. And while we were still trying to make a plan, the cheaper tickets kept selling out because of the National Day holiday.

Eventually, I started accepting that maybe this trip simply wasn't going to happen.

Then suddenly, the day to leave Singapore arrived.

And my husband looked at me and said,

“What do we have to lose? Let's go.”

That was it.

No more overthinking.

One Bag, No Plan, and Just ChatGPT

We packed a few clothes, left everything else behind, and walked out.

No hotel booking.

No bus tickets.

No proper itinerary.

No idea where we would stay.

Our only real travel planner was ChatGPT. 😂

We reached Woodlands MRT and somehow found a bus to Johor Bahru.

From Woodlands Checkpoint, we crossed into Malaysia, cleared immigration, and within less than two hours of making the decision, we were officially in another country.

And then reality hit.

We were in Johor Bahru.

But our actual destination was Kuala Lumpur.

Would we even find a bus?

Somehow, We Kept Finding Our Way

We got on a bus toward Larkin Central.

And somewhere along the way, we suddenly remembered something very important:

We needed Malaysian cash.

So we stopped to withdraw money and, of course, lost a painful amount in fees and conversion.

But by then, there was no point worrying.

The adventure had already started.

The bus was playing Hindi songs, and we were sitting there, watching the scenery outside and laughing at how ridiculous our situation was.

Eventually, we reached the bus station and managed to get tickets to Kuala Lumpur for around 15 SGD.

Fifteen.

For a journey to Kuala Lumpur.

We looked at each other like, Okay, maybe this trip is actually going to work.

We grabbed some food from McDonald's, got back on the bus, and spent most of the journey sleeping.

And then, finally—

Kuala Lumpur.

Finding a Place to Sleep in Bukit Bintang

Of course, we had forgotten one small detail.

We had nowhere to stay.

We hadn't booked a hotel beforehand, and our budget was limited.

So we started looking around Bukit Bintang and eventually found a room at oA ThE FACTORY.

It wasn't luxurious.

It didn't need to be.

The place was cool, comfortable enough, and for the price we were paying, it honestly felt like the best deal we could have found.

And somewhere around this point, my husband officially fell in love with Malaysia.

I think the food had something to do with it. 😂

Our First Morning in Kuala Lumpur

The next morning, we woke up and headed out for Jummah.

After that, we started our actual exploration of Kuala Lumpur.

Our first destination was the one everyone told us we had to see:

The Petronas Twin Towers.

Standing there and looking up at those enormous towers felt strangely surreal.

Just a day earlier, we weren't even sure if we could afford to come.

And now we were standing in the middle of Kuala Lumpur.

After that, we walked through Saloma Link and made our way toward Jalan Alor in Bukit Bintang.

And then Malaysia started winning us over in the best possible way—

the food.

Eating Our Way Through Kuala Lumpur

One thing we quickly realized was how much food we could try without spending too much.

We started with shawarma from Damascus.

Then Chinese beef roti.

Then drinks.

Then another food stop.

Then something else we had never tried before.

At some point, the trip became less about following a destination list and more about simply walking around, finding food, trying things, and enjoying the city together.

And then, as if the universe was finally taking our side, our salary came into the card.

So yes—

the food exploration continued. 😂

By the end of the day, we were exhausted, completely full, and very happy.

We went back and slept.

Thean Hou Temple, Merdeka Square and a Little Peace

The next day, our first destination was Thean Hou Temple.

The red and white architecture was beautiful, and the whole place had such a distinct atmosphere. We spent some time walking around, taking photos, and simply enjoying how different everything felt.

From there, we headed toward Merdeka Square.

We visited Masjid Jamek and I prayed there.

And honestly, that moment stayed with me.

There was something incredibly peaceful about standing inside the mosque after spending the whole day surrounded by crowds, traffic, food, noise and movement.

For a few minutes, everything felt still.

When we came outside, there was a huge crowd because the King was arriving.

So naturally, we stayed around to see what was happening.

We watched, explored, enjoyed the crowd, and somehow ended up doing quite a bit of shopping too.

Malaysia was definitely becoming dangerous for our budget. 😂

Batu Caves and the Road to Genting

The next day brought another adventure.

We started with Batu Caves.

Then we headed toward Genting Highlands.

As the bus slowly climbed higher and higher, I remember thinking that maybe, just maybe, this day was going to be completely smooth.

No missed buses.

No booking problems.

No unexpected expenses.

Just a peaceful little adventure.

Then we took the cable car up.

The views were beautiful, the ride itself was amazing, and we explored Genting for a while.

For a trip that had started with absolutely no plan, everything seemed to be going surprisingly well.

Which, of course, meant something had to go wrong next.

Because Apparently, Our Trip Needed a Little Drama

When we came back down from Genting, we discovered that there were no bus tickets left.

Everything was booked.

And taking a car would cost around 100 SGD.

For two people travelling on a very limited budget, that was definitely not happening.

So we did what we had been doing throughout the entire trip.

We improvised.

We found another couple and managed to share a ride.

Somehow, we made our way back toward Johor Bahru.

At this point, we were tired but also laughing at ourselves.

Because every time we thought, Okay, now everything is sorted, another problem appeared.

And then we reached Johor Bahru.

Only to discover—

there was no bus to Singapore until after 4 PM.

Of course.

One Last Taste of Malaysia

So we waited.

We walked around the bus station.

We ate.

And ate again.

We tried some of the food we hadn't had a chance to try earlier and bought a few things to take back to Singapore.

We also managed to do a little more shopping, because apparently even an almost-empty budget couldn't completely stop us.

Eventually, our bus arrived.

And just like that, our completely unplanned Malaysia adventure was coming to an end.
